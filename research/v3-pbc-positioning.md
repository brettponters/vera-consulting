## 3. Narrative Structures (4 Distinct Patterns)

**A. Lead with work / receipts (Nava, Bridgespan, Dalberg).** The hero is a recent piece of work, an article, or a number that proves track record. The mission is referenced obliquely.

**B. Lead with thesis / worldview (Civilla, Acumen, Reboot).** A short, declarative belief statement opens the page. The thesis filters who reads on.

**C. Lead with method / how-we-work (Reos, Truss, Greater Good).** Hero focuses on the way of working. Mission is downstream of method.

**D. Lead with the audience's problem (Truss secondary, Nava metrics).** "Overcome what's holding you back," followed by named pains. Audience-first; the firm is the resolution.

The pattern RAIN should likely adopt is a hybrid of **B + A**: a one-line worldview hero, immediately backed by work/structural commitments rather than more rhetoric.

## 4. How They Handle the Mission Claim Without Preaching

- **Name the structural commitment once, then move on.** Nava says "public benefit corporation" once or twice on About, then writes about service outcomes. Truss never mentions PBC on its homepage at all.
- **Substitute concrete numbers for adjectives.** Nava: "over 50 million households," "three billion in payments." Dalberg: "1,600+ leaders, 4,000+ engagements, 148 countries."
- **Pick a specific audience-shaped frame, not a universal one.** Greater Good's subhead names its audience ("a Design Firm Dedicated to the Social Sector") in one line.
- **Talk about the work, not the worker.** Strong mission firms describe what's *being built* and *who's served*. Weak ones describe their own attributes ("passionate," "purpose-driven").
- **Use plainness as a credibility signal.** Truss: "We're builders. We build great software and teams."
- **Let the client list and case studies argue the mission.**

## 5. Anti-Patterns

- **Mission-as-aesthetic.** FSG's edison-bulb hero imagery — visual metaphor doing work the copy should do.
- **Stacking abstractions.** Reos: "supports leaders and institutions to face and address the challenges of our time" — three abstractions in one sentence.
- **Self-congratulatory verbs.** "We empower," "we catalyze," "we ignite." None of the credible operators use these.
- **The universal-good claim.** Dalberg's "all people, everywhere, can reach their fullest potential" — signals nothing because it excludes nothing.
- **Performative inclusivity language with no operational referent.**
- **Manifestos in lieu of work.** Sites that lead with a values page or a long mission essay before showing any client work read as untested.

## 6. Five Takeaways for RAIN

1. **Lead the homepage with a thesis sentence, not a mission claim.** Something shaped like Acumen's "Investing in dignity" — a short worldview line that filters the reader. Do not open with "we are a public benefit corporation."

2. **Name the PBC charter and % commitment exactly once, in a single concrete sentence.** Treat it the way Nava treats PBC status: stated, anchored, never repeated.

3. **Replace every adjective with a number, a client type, or a verb.** Truss's "We're builders. We build great software and teams." is the model — five words, zero posture.

4. **Define the audience in the subhead, not the body.** Greater Good's "Design Firm Dedicated to the Social Sector" is doing real work in nine words. Name the industries (finance, healthcare, legal, regulated B2B) in the first screen.

5. **Let the work and the structure carry the mission. Never argue for it.**
