# Naming Research: AI Consultancy Brand Candidates

> Organized by approach lane. Each candidate includes: name, why it works, aesthetic fit, domain situation, closest collision.

---

## Lane 1: Stripe-Class Single English Words (Old Trades / Unusual but Specific)

### 1. Pawl
- **Why it works**: A pawl is the small pivoting catch in a ratchet that allows movement in only one direction -- prevents backsliding. Metaphor: you help companies move forward with AI and lock in progress. One syllable, hard consonant ending, feels mechanical and precise. Nobody uses this word outside mechanical engineering.
- **Aesthetic fit**: Excellent. Four letters, dark-mode friendly, looks sharp in Cabinet Grotesk caps. P-A-W-L has the same visual density as "Pika" or "Hex."
- **Domain**: pawl.ai likely available (no tech company found). pawl.com would need acquisition. pawl.co or pawl.dev viable alternatives.
- **Closest collision**: No tech company uses this name. Only appears in industrial parts catalogs.

### 2. Selvage
- **Why it works**: The self-finished edge of woven fabric that prevents unraveling. Metaphor: you build AI systems with clean, finished edges -- nothing frays. From weaving, one of humanity's oldest technologies. Three syllables but flows naturally. Implies completeness and craft.
- **Aesthetic fit**: Strong. Textile connotation feels warm and human (not cold-tech). Works for the "responsible AI" angle -- things that don't unravel.
- **Domain**: selvage.ai likely available. selvage.com is a small UK craft business (acquirable). "Selvedge" (alternate spelling) has more collisions (VC firm, magazine).
- **Closest collision**: Selvage.co.uk (tiny craft business in Scotland). Selvedge Venture (London VC) uses the alternate spelling.

### 3. Tenon
- **Why it works**: A projecting piece of wood in a mortise-and-tenon joint -- the oldest, strongest joinery method. Metaphor: you build AI that fits precisely into existing business structures. Two syllables, clean, premium feel. The tenon is the part that DOES the joining -- the active piece.
- **Aesthetic fit**: Very strong. Five letters, balanced, looks excellent in geometric sans. Has the same weight as "Vercel" or "Notion."
- **Domain**: tenon.ai likely available. tenon.com not available. tenonhq.com taken (ServiceNow marketing tool). tenon.co potentially available.
- **Closest collision**: Tenon HQ (marketing automation on ServiceNow -- different space). Tenon Ventures (deep tech VC -- adjacent but not competing).

### 4. Quoin
- **Why it works**: Pronounced "coin." A cornerstone block at the angle of a building -- literally the structural piece that holds two walls together. From both masonry and printing (the wedge that locks type in a press). Metaphor: the foundational piece that holds your AI strategy together.
- **Aesthetic fit**: Good. Five letters, unusual spelling creates memorability. The Q gives it visual distinction in any typeface.
- **Domain**: quoin.ai likely available. quoin.com taken by a boutique tech firm (since 1994, possibly acquirable). quoin.co possible.
- **Closest collision**: Quoin Inc (boutique web/mobile dev firm, small). Quoin Pharma (NASDAQ: QNRX, different space entirely).

### 5. Fuller
- **Why it works**: A blacksmithing tool -- a rounded hammer used to shape grooves and curves in metal. Also a textile trade: a fuller shrinks and thickens wool cloth. Metaphor: you shape raw AI capabilities into something refined and functional. Two syllables, familiar word but most people don't know the trade meaning.
- **Aesthetic fit**: Warm, approachable, has weight. Looks grounded in a serif or geometric sans.
- **Domain**: fuller.ai likely needs acquisition. fuller.com taken. Could work as a longer domain (usefuller.ai, etc.) but the standalone is strong.
- **Closest collision**: Fuller (common surname). Buckminster Fuller associations (architecture/design -- actually good). H.B. Fuller (adhesives company -- different world).

## Lane 2: Two-Word Constructions (Not "X Labs")

### 6. Quiet Engine
- **Why it works**: Contradictory pairing that's immediately memorable. AI is the engine; your approach is quiet (no hype, no buzzwords, just results). Captures the "responsible/measured" angle without preaching. Evokes power held in restraint.
- **Aesthetic fit**: Excellent for dark-mode. "QUIET ENGINE" in tight-tracked Cabinet Grotesk on black = premium. The contradiction creates visual tension.
- **Domain**: quietengine.com likely available. quietengine.ai likely available.
- **Closest collision**: No exact match found. "Quiet" is used by some meditation apps. "Engine" appears in many startup names but not paired this way.

### 7. Slowglass
- **Why it works**: From Bob Shaw's sci-fi story "Light of Other Days" -- glass so thick that light takes years to pass through it, capturing entire scenes. Metaphor: AI that sees the full picture, not just the surface. Deep reference that rewards those who catch it. Science fiction heritage signals futurism without being literal.
- **Aesthetic fit**: Beautiful. Nine letters, one compound word. Feels like it could be a Scandinavian design studio. The "slow" prefix signals deliberateness in a fast-moving field.
- **Domain**: slowglass.com likely available. slowglass.ai likely available.
- **Closest collision**: No tech company found. Term exists in SF literary circles but has no brand presence.

### 8. Northpaper
- **Why it works**: Evokes cartography (navigation charts were drawn on paper, north at top). Implies direction + documentation. Suggests you help companies map their AI territory. Unusual compound that feels like it should exist but doesn't.
- **Aesthetic fit**: Strong editorial vibe. Works for the "consultant as mapmaker" positioning. Looks premium in a serif font.
- **Domain**: northpaper.com likely available. northpaper.ai likely available.
- **Closest collision**: No exact match found. "North" appears in many company names but not paired this way.

## Lane 3: Non-Western Language Words (Pronounceable by English Speakers)

### 9. Takumi
- **Why it works**: Japanese (匠) -- master craftsman whose skills are revered even by other craftsmen. Toyota uses "Takumi" for its elite hand-builders (only ~8 certified at any time). Three syllables, fully pronounceable (tah-KOO-mee), beautiful sound. Implies: this is not a factory operation, this is a master's craft.
- **Aesthetic fit**: Strong. Six letters, balanced. The Japanese origin aligns with "precision without pretension." Works in Latin typefaces.
- **Domain**: takumi.ai likely needs acquisition (common Japanese name). takumi.co may be available. Could use gettakumi.com or similar.
- **Closest collision**: Wabi Takumi (Japanese tableware brand). Toyota's Takumi program (not a company, a certification -- actually reinforces the meaning).

### 10. Varsel
- **Why it works**: Norwegian/Swedish -- means "notice," "warning," "alert" (as in a signal that something important is coming). Pronounced VAR-sel. Two syllables, feels like a real English word but isn't. For AI safety/responsible AI: the one who gives fair warning, who sees what's coming.
- **Aesthetic fit**: Excellent. Six letters, close to "Vercel" in visual weight but semantically different. Looks native in Nordic-inspired dark design.
- **Domain**: varsel.ai likely available. varsel.com may be available (Scandinavian word, less likely taken by English-language companies).
- **Closest collision**: Varsel is a Norwegian weather alert app ("Yr Varsel"). No English-language tech company found with this name.

### 11. Smed
- **Why it works**: Danish/Norwegian/Swedish -- means "smith" (as in blacksmith). Pronounced exactly as written. One syllable, four letters. The smith metaphor: you forge AI tools from raw material. "Smed" is sharper and more novel than "Smith."
- **Aesthetic fit**: Extremely strong. Four letters, monosyllabic, looks incredible in uppercase geometric sans. SMED. Has the same visual impact as "DEPT" or "STRP."
- **Domain**: smed.ai likely available. smed.com likely needs acquisition (common Scandinavian surname). smed.co possible.
- **Closest collision**: SMED (manufacturing term: "Single-Minute Exchange of Die" -- a lean manufacturing concept, which actually has a nice overlap with efficiency). Various people named Smed.

## Lane 4: Abstract / Made-Up but Pronounceable

### 12. Venn
- **Why it works**: Technically a real word (Venn diagram) but functions as abstract. Two overlapping circles = the intersection of AI capability and business need. Four letters, one syllable, everyone already knows how to spell and pronounce it. The diagram metaphor is perfect: you find the overlap.
- **Aesthetic fit**: Clean, geometric, minimal. Four letters in geometric sans = designer catnip. V-E-N-N has symmetry.
- **Domain**: venn.ai likely taken (common word). venn.co taken. Would need creative domain (usevenn.com, vennhq.com).
- **Closest collision**: Several companies use Venn (Venn Technology, Venn diagram tools). More crowded than other candidates.

### 13. Tyn
- **Why it works**: Three letters, one syllable. Looks like it should mean something. Phonetically related to "tin" (metalwork) and "tyne" (the prong of a fork -- pointed, precise). Short enough to be a logo and a word simultaneously. Pronounced "tin."
- **Aesthetic fit**: Exceptional. Three letters = maximum logo potential. TYN in tight spacing looks like a monogram. Works at any size.
- **Domain**: tyn.ai likely available. tyn.com likely taken (three-letter .com). tyn.co possible.
- **Closest collision**: No major tech company. "Tyn" appears as a surname and in some Eastern European contexts (Tyn church in Prague).

### 14. Cove
- **Why it works**: Four letters, one syllable, everyone knows it. A cove is a sheltered bay -- safe harbor. Metaphor: you provide safe passage through AI transformation. Also: a concave architectural molding, from carpentry. Warm and protective without being preachy about safety.
- **Aesthetic fit**: Soft but not weak. Works in both serif and sans. The "ov" center gives it warmth.
- **Domain**: cove.ai may be taken. cove.co taken. Would need creative extension.
- **Closest collision**: Cove is used by several companies (insurance, coworking). More crowded.

## Lane 5: Responsible / Careful / Measured Angle (Old Craft Words)

### 15. Plumb
- **Why it works**: A plumb line tells you what's truly vertical -- what's true. From Latin "plumbum" (lead). Carpenters and builders use it to ensure structures are straight and honest. Metaphor: you keep AI implementations honest, aligned, true. One syllable, five letters, the "b" ending gives it gravity.
- **Aesthetic fit**: Strong. Old-world craft word that doesn't feel archaic. "PLUMB" in a bold grotesk on dark background = conviction.
- **Domain**: plumb.ai likely available. plumb.com taken (plumbing companies). plumb.co possible.
- **Closest collision**: Plumb (the singer). Various plumbing companies. No tech company found.

### 16. Adze
- **Why it works**: A precision hand tool for shaping wood -- curved blade, used to carefully remove material to create a smooth surface. Metaphor: you refine and shape raw AI into something usable. Four letters, one syllable, the "dz" ending is distinctive and memorable. Obscure enough to be ownable.
- **Aesthetic fit**: Very strong. Four letters with an unusual letter combo. ADZE looks sharp and intentional in any typeface. The Z gives it edge.
- **Domain**: adze.ai likely available (only collision is Adze Biotech in a completely different space). adze.co likely available.
- **Closest collision**: Adze Biotechnology (cancer immunotherapy, Coral Gables FL). Completely different domain, no confusion.

### 17. Anglage
- **Why it works**: A watchmaking finishing technique -- hand-beveling and polishing the edges of movement components to exact 45-degree angles. The highest expression of watchmaking craft. Pronounced "ON-glazh." Metaphor: you bring finishing-level craft to AI work, not just functionality but polish. Three syllables, French origin, sounds premium.
- **Aesthetic fit**: Luxury tier. The French watchmaking origin maps directly to "premium consultant who charges accordingly." Looks beautiful in italics or light-weight type.
- **Domain**: anglage.ai likely available. anglage.com likely available. No tech companies found using this name.
- **Closest collision**: None found in tech. The word exists only in watchmaking literature. Highly ownable.

## Lane 6: Wildcards

### 18. Detent
- **Why it works**: The mechanism that holds something in a fixed position until deliberately released. In watchmaking, the detent escapement is the most precise type. Metaphor: you help companies hold position (stability) while ensuring the right moment to release (deploy AI). Two syllables, six letters. Technical enough to signal expertise, English enough to be pronounceable.
- **Aesthetic fit**: Sharp and mechanical. "DETENT" in monospace or geometric sans feels like a precision instrument brand. Has a Vercel/Linear vibe.
- **Domain**: detent.ai likely available. detent.com likely available. No tech companies found.
- **Closest collision**: None in tech. Only appears in mechanical engineering contexts.

### 19. Colophon
- **Why it works**: The inscription at the end of a book identifying the printer, typeface, paper, and production details -- a maker's mark of craft. In publishing, the colophon IS the signal that someone cared about how things were made. Metaphor: you're the kind of builder who signs their work. Four syllables but flows beautifully (COL-oh-fon).
- **Aesthetic fit**: Perfect for the typography-as-design approach from the design research. A brand named Colophon should have impeccable type. The word itself is a design insider's reference.
- **Domain**: colophon.ai likely available. colophon.com likely taken. colophon.co possible.
- **Closest collision**: Colophon Foundry (type design studio, acquired by Monotype 2023). This is both a collision AND a validation -- being in the same semantic space as a respected type foundry reinforces the craft angle.

### 20. Raker
- **Why it works**: In sawmill terminology, the raker tooth is the one that clears the kerf (the cut) -- it doesn't cut new ground, it clears the path for what comes next. Also: one who rakes, who gathers. Metaphor: you clear the path for AI adoption. Two syllables, five letters, active and muscular. The "-er" suffix implies a doer, not a thinker.
- **Aesthetic fit**: Action-oriented. Feels like a tool brand (which is good for a consulting practice -- you're a tool, not a pundit). Works in bold sans.
- **Domain**: raker.ai likely available. raker.com likely taken (common word). raker.co possible.
- **Closest collision**: Raker-Roberta's (plant nursery). No tech company found.
