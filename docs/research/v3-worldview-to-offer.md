# RAIN Research: Worldview-to-Offer Page Architecture

## 1. Firms — Structural Sketches

**IDEO — ideo.com**
- Hero: "Human-centered design" + rotating noun. Three CTAs all pointing to /design-services: "Build your next era of growth" / "Design human-centered strategies" / "Get to market with confidence"
- Scroll 2: "Latest" — three editorial cards (worldview)
- Scroll 3: Featured Work grid (Sephora, H&M, Ford, Boom, Moderna)
- Scroll 4: David Kelley quote + restrained CTA "Ask us for references"
- Pattern: worldview owns the hero, but the hero CTAs already say "we'll come do this for you." Offer is implicit in verbs, not a "Services" panel.

**frog — frog.co**
- Hero: "Advancing the human experience through design"
- Scroll 1: Carousel of editorial
- Scroll 2: "Welcome to the pond" — studios/people
- Scroll 3: "Our work" grid (Unilever, IKEA, Volvo, Sanofi, JPMorgan)
- Scroll 4: "Our principles" — four pillars
- Footer prompt: "Ready to make your mark?" → "Get in touch"
- Pattern: worldview-only homepage. Offer arrives only in the footer. Services live on a separate page.

**Reos Partners — reospartners.com** (Closest analogue to RAIN)
- Hero: "Transforming Systems, Together" + 20-year track record line
- Scroll 1: Why-now problem framing + "Let's Connect" / "Explore Our Solutions" CTAs
- Scroll 2: Three solution shapes — Transformative Platforms / Strategic Futures / Leadership Capabilities
- Scroll 3: Five sector cards
- Scroll 4: Testimonials
- Pattern: Worldview hero → problem framing → three engagement shapes inline (no prices, no tiers) → sector lenses → proof.

**Nava PBC — navapbc.com** (Closest PBC analogue)
- Hero: "Making government services simple and effective" + "See our work"
- Scroll 1: "What's New" — 2025 Public Benefit Report
- Scroll 2: Impact metrics (50M households, $3B disbursed, 500k hours)
- Scroll 3: "Supporting Structural Change" — three capability cards: "Building services / Helping programs / Making agencies"
- Scroll 4: "A Trusted Partner" — agency logos
- Pattern: No "hire us" CTA above fold. The offer is the work itself + capability triad. PBC report featured as worldview signal. Engagement happens through "See our work" → case studies, not a contact form.

**Greater Good Studio — greatergoodstudio.com**
- Hero: "People at the center of change." / "Greater Good Studio is a Design Firm Dedicated to the Social Sector."
- Scroll 1: Six featured projects
- Scroll 2: "Working together" — partnership philosophy section
- Scroll 3: "A great team" → "Meet Our Team"
- Scroll 4: Press / testimonials + co-founder availability "Schedule a Chat"
- Pattern: Offer enters as a *philosophy* section ("Working together") not a services list. Commercial mechanism is "schedule a chat with a founder."

**Anthropic — anthropic.com**
- Hero: "AI research and products that put safety at the frontier" / subhead names PBC status and dual mission
- Scroll 1: Project Glasswing feature
- Scroll 2: Latest releases (three product/research cards)
- Scroll 3: Five core values cards
- Pattern: Homepage is *entirely* worldview. The "engage" path lives only in nav ("Contact sales") and footer.

## 2. The Transition — Where the Offer Enters

Three patterns observed:

- **Hero verbs (offer baked into worldview)** — IDEO, Stripe. The headline names what they do for you.
- **Second/third scroll capability triad** — Reos, Nava, frog (services page). Three shapes, one sentence each.
- **Footer-only / "philosophy" section** — frog homepage, Greater Good, Anthropic. Offer is implicit; engagement is "get in touch" or "schedule a chat."

Direct excerpts of "engage with us" language:

- IDEO hero: "Build your next era of growth" / "Design human-centered strategies" / "Get to market with confidence"
- IDEO mid-page: "Ask us for references"
- frog footer: "Ready to make your mark?" → "Get in touch"
- Reos hero: "Let's Connect" / "Explore Our Solutions"
- Greater Good: "Working together" + "Schedule a Chat" with a co-founder
- Bain: "Let's Get to Work"
- Nava: "See our work" (the offer is the work, not a contact form)
- Anthropic nav: "Contact sales"

## 3. Restrained CTAs — Phrasing Patterns

Most restrained → most salesy:

- "Say hello" (IDEO header)
- "Get in touch" (frog footer)
- "Let's Connect" (Reos)
- "Ask us for references" (IDEO — exceptionally restrained; assumes seriousness)
- "Schedule a Chat" (Greater Good — names a person)
- "Working together" / "Learn More" (section as CTA)
- "Let's Get to Work" (Bain)
- "Contact sales" (Anthropic)

The "trusted family doctor" register clusters around: *"Say hello"*, *"Get in touch"*, *"Ask us for references"*, *"Schedule a chat"*. Avoid: "Get started," "Sign up," "Book a demo," "Talk to an expert."

## 4. Engagement-Shape Disclosure

Closest analogues for RAIN's Strategy / Build / Steward:

- **Reos Partners** — three side-by-side cards on homepage, each with a one-sentence verb-led description. No prices. (Transformative Platforms / Strategic Futures / Leadership Capabilities)
- **Nava PBC** — three capability cards: "Building services / Helping programs / Making agencies" — verb-led, parallel structure, no tiers
- **frog** — services live on a separate page entirely
- **IDEO** — services as a destination page; homepage uses three *outcome* CTAs

Dominant pattern for mission-led firms: **three parallel verb-led one-liners**, no prices, no tiers, no feature comparison.

## 5. Selected Work Placement

- **Above the offer**: IDEO, Clay, Nava (the work *is* the offer)
- **Below the offer**: Reos
- **Inline with offer**: Bain

Dominant pattern: **work above the offer**. The case studies do the persuading; the offer is then a small, restrained action at the bottom.

## 6. Anti-Patterns

- **SaaS pricing tables on a consulting page.** Ladder-style "Starter / Pro / Enterprise" cards collapse "we will come into your business" into "pick a plan."
- **Stripe-style hero CTA.** "Get started" / "Sign up with Google" works for product but reads transactional for consulting.
- **Vincit-style** "DO YOU EXPECT EXTRAORDINARY BUSINESS RESULTS? We Do." — opposite of family-doctor register.
- **Anthropic-style total burial.** Works for a research lab with inbound demand RAIN does not yet have.
- **Range.studio's** single email button — too cool to hire.
- **Stripe / 21st.dev** "Get started" + "Documentation" reads as self-serve product, not consulting.

## 7. Five Takeaways for RAIN

1. **Bake the offer into the hero verbs.** IDEO's "Build your next era of growth" is a worldview *and* an offer. RAIN's hero should name what RAIN does for a client (verb-led).

2. **Use Reos/Nava-style three parallel one-liners for Strategy / Build / Steward.** Three cards, verb-led, one sentence each, no prices, no "learn more" buttons that imply tiering. Place them on the second or third scroll.

3. **Put selected work above the engagement ask.** Case studies persuade; the ask should be quiet. Pattern: worldview hero → three shapes → selected work → small "Say hello" CTA in footer.

4. **CTA register: "Say hello," "Get in touch," "Ask us for references," "Schedule a chat."** Avoid: "Get started," "Book a demo," "Talk to sales." Greater Good's "Schedule a Chat" naming a co-founder is the most family-doctor-like signal observed.

5. **Use the PBC status the way Anthropic and Nava do — as a worldview anchor on the homepage, not a footer disclosure.** Anthropic names PBC structure in the hero subhead; Nava features the Public Benefit Report as the first card after the hero.

**The structural pattern that fits RAIN:** Hero (worldview + verb-led offer in one) → Why-now / problem framing → Three shapes (Strategy / Build / Steward) → Selected work grid → Quiet "Get in touch" / founder-availability section → Footer. **Reos + Nava + IDEO hybrid, Anthropic-grade restraint on the ask.**
